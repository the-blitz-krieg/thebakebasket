Celebration cakes

Celebration cakes, baked for one day: yours

A good birthday cake has two jobs. It has to make them gasp when the box opens, and it has to make them go quiet when they take the first bite. Plenty of cakes manage the first. I bake for the second.

Every cake starts with a conversation, not a catalogue. Who is it for? How old are they turning? Do they pick the cherries off the Black Forest, or steal everyone else's? Then I bake it for that person, on the day, with real cream and real chocolate, and confirm every detail, and the price, in writing before I start.

For every kind of celebration

Children's birthdays. Unicorns, dinosaurs, cricket pitches, their favourite colour in a ridiculous quantity. Theme cakes that still taste like cake, because the six-year-old will eat the frosting and the grandparents will eat the sponge, and both should be happy.

First birthdays. Soft, simple sponges with less sugar on request, and smash cakes small enough for tiny hands. Read: Choosing a First Birthday Cake: Smash Cakes, Flavours and Sugar

Milestone birthdays and anniversaries. Amma's 60th, your parents' 40th, the colleague who's finally retiring. Elegant floral buttercream, tiered cakes, or one perfect chocolate truffle with their name in a steady hand.

Weddings, engagements, baby showers, baptisms and First Communions. Tiered and fondant cakes, planned with you well ahead of the day.

The ordinary Tuesday. You don't need a reason. A small cake for no occasion at all is one of the nicest things you can do for a house.

Flavours people come back for

The classics are classics for a reason. My chocolate truffle is dark, glossy and properly rich, with a sponge that stays moist right to the last slice the next day. The butterscotch has crunch in every forkful. The pineapple tastes of fruit, not of a bottle.

Then there are the ones with a little Chennai in them. Filter-coffee mocha, with real decoction folded into the cream, so it smells like a Mylapore kitchen at seven in the morning. Tender coconut, light and milky, for the hot months. Rasmalai and gulab jamun cakes for the family that wants mithai and cake and refuses to choose.

Red velvet with a tangy cream cheese frosting. Salted caramel that makes people close their eyes. Biscoff, hazelnut praline, pistachio rose. Ask me which one fits the person and I'll tell you honestly.

Which frosting should you choose?

Finish Best for Keep in mind
Fresh whipped cream Light, classic cakes; most children's flavours Must stay refrigerated; softens quickly in Chennai heat
Buttercream Floral designs, piping, richer flavours Holds shape better, richer taste
Cream cheese Red velvet, carrot, some fruit cakes Tangy, refrigerate
Ganache Chocolate cakes, drip cakes Holds well, intensely chocolatey
Fondant Sculpted themes, sharp edges, figurines Mostly for looks; many people peel it off. Not ideal in very humid weather

How big a cake do you need?

A rough guide for party-sized slices:

Cake weight Serves
Bento (about 300 to 400 g) 2 to 4
500 g 5 to 6
1 kg 10 to 12
1.5 kg 15 to 18
2 kg 20 to 25
3 kg (often tiered) 30 to 35

If dinner is heavy and it's mostly adults, you can stretch it. If there are twelve children and a piñata, don't. Read: How Much Cake Do You Need? A Serving Guide for Every Party Size

FAQs

Questions people ask

How many days in advance should I order a custom cake in Chennai?

Please order cakes at least 2 days ahead. Tiered, detailed fondant and wedding cakes need longer, so message as soon as you have a date. During festival weeks and December, book as early as you can.

Can you copy a cake design from a picture?

Priya can create a cake inspired by your picture, adjusted to the size you need and to what will hold up well. She won't copy another baker's signature design exactly, but she'll make it your own.

Do you make cartoon and character theme cakes?

Yes, theme cakes are made with your child's favourite colours, hobbies and ideas. Where a design uses a trademarked character, Priya will suggest a theme inspired by it, such as colours and props, rather than a copy.

What is the best cake flavour for a kids' birthday party?

Chocolate truffle, vanilla with fresh fruit, and butterscotch are the safest crowd-pleasers. For very young children, a lighter vanilla or fruit sponge with less sugar goes down well with parents too.

Can I get a cake with less sugar?

Yes. Many cakes can be made with reduced sugar, and there are jaggery and date-sweetened cakes on the healthy menu. A reduced-sugar cake still contains sugar, so please don't treat it as diabetic-safe.

Will the cake survive the drive across Chennai?

Cream cakes are packed cold in sturdy boxes and should travel flat on the car floor with the AC on, not on a seat or a lap. Buttercream and ganache cakes hold up better in the heat than fresh cream.
