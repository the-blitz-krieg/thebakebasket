Christmas Food in Chennai's Kerala and Tamil Christian Homes

BBy Beth MathewPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Christmas in Kerala and Tamil Christian homes is defined as much by food as by the star on the balcony. In Kerala homes: plum cake, appam with stew or a spicy meat curry, vattayappam, rose cookies (achappam) and kuzhalappam, and homemade wine. In Tamil Christian homes: plum cake, biryani for the Christmas lunch, kalkal (kulkuls), rose cookies and sweets shared with neighbours. In Chennai, the two traditions often sit on the same table.

The weeks before

Christmas food starts early. The fruit for the plum cake goes into its jar in October or November. Grapes are crushed for homemade wine weeks ahead. In the last fortnight, the kitchen turns into a factory of fried snacks: achappam, kuzhalappam and kalkal are made in quantities that seem absurd, because they're meant to be given away.

The tradition of sharing is central. Plates of sweets go to Hindu and Muslim neighbours, who will send plates back at Deepavali and Eid. In many Chennai apartment blocks, those plates are how people who barely speak the rest of the year end up knowing each other's children's names.

The Kerala table

Appam and stew. Lacy-edged, soft-centred appam with a mild, creamy stew of chicken, mutton or vegetables cooked in coconut milk with whole spices. Christmas breakfast in many homes.

Vattayappam. The steamed cousin of appam, sweet and soft, often eaten with the meat curry. More in what is vattayappam.

Duck roast, beef fry, meen moilee. The heavy hitters for lunch, depending on the family and the region.

Plum cake. Dark, rich, cut into thick slices and offered to every visitor.

Achappam and kuzhalappam. Achappam (rose cookies) are crisp, lacy flowers made with a special iron mould dipped in batter and fried. Kuzhalappam are crunchy rolled tubes. Both are made in big batches and stored in tins.

Homemade wine. Grape wine made at home, though many families now make a non-alcoholic grape juice version.

The Tamil Christian table

Biryani. In many Tamil Christian homes in and around Chennai, Christmas lunch means biryani, often mutton, with raita and a brinjal curry.

Kalkal (kulkuls). Small curled dough pieces, fried and coated in sugar. Children traditionally help shape them on the back of a fork.

Rose cookies. Shared with Kerala homes.

Plum cake. Bought from a favourite bakery or made at home.

Sweets for neighbours. Plates of kalkal, rose cookies, cake and murukku, carried door to door.

When you're far from home

For many families who've moved to Chennai from Kerala or the southern districts, Christmas is the time they miss home most. Grandmothers who made achappam are far away, or gone. The jar of fruit isn't on the shelf. The working week runs right up to the 24th.

It's completely fine to order some of it. A plum cake and vattayappam from someone who makes them carefully, the same way, can bring the house back to how it used to smell. FAQs

Questions people ask

What is the traditional Christmas food in Kerala?

Traditional Kerala Christmas food includes plum cake, appam with stew, vattayappam, duck roast or beef fry, achappam (rose cookies), kuzhalappam and homemade wine.

What do Tamil Christians eat at Christmas?

Many Tamil Christian families have biryani for Christmas lunch, along with plum cake, kalkal, rose cookies and sweets shared with neighbours.

When should I order Christmas plum cake in Chennai?

Book Christmas plum cake by early to mid November. Bakers make limited batches, and December fills up quickly.
