What to Send Someone in Hospital (Food That Actually Helps)

BBy Beth MathewPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Before you send food to someone in hospital, ask the family what the patient is allowed to eat; many patients are on restricted diets. If they can eat normally, send small amounts of gentle, mild food: soft bread, plain cake, idiyappam, rusks, fruit. Often the most useful thing is food for the family sitting by the bed, who are surviving on canteen coffee.

That last point is the one people forget. We build care baskets around it.

Check first

Hospitals are not the place for surprises. A patient might be fasting before a procedure, on a diabetic diet, a low-salt diet, a soft diet, or nothing by mouth at all. Some hospitals don't allow outside food in certain wards.

Message a family member: "Is there anything he can eat? Would the family like some food?" It's a kind question, and it saves wasted food and awkward moments.

If the patient can eat normally

Choose food that's:

  • Gentle. Mild, not spicy, not oily.
  • Small. A few pieces, not a 1 kg cake. Appetite is usually low.
  • Easy to eat in bed. No crumbs everywhere, no sauces, no knives.
  • Not too sweet. Very sweet food can be unappealing when someone's unwell.
  • Easy to store. Hospital rooms rarely have a fridge.

Good choices: soft milk bread or rusks, a plain tea cake or banana bread, idiyappam with mild coconut milk, soft idlis, fresh fruit that's easy to eat (bananas, oranges, grapes), plain biscuits, homemade kanji if you know they'll like it.

Avoid: cream cakes, heavily spiced food, fried snacks, strong-smelling food (other patients share the room), and anything that needs reheating.

For someone recovering at home

This is where food really helps. Someone home after surgery or a long illness often can't cook, and their family is exhausted. Useful things:

  • Soft bread and a loaf of whole-wheat or multigrain for breakfasts
  • Idiyappam, puttu or idli batter for easy meals
  • A mild cake or banana bread for visitors
  • Soups or kanji, if you're close enough to bring them warm
  • Fruit

Send it in small quantities, more than once, rather than everything in one day.

For the family by the bed

When someone's in hospital, the people around them stop eating properly. They live on canteen samosas, tea and whatever's in the vending machine. A basket for them, quietly delivered, is often more appreciated than anything for the patient.

  • Sandwiches or savoury buns
  • Brownies or cookies for energy
  • Muffins
  • Fruit
  • A flask of something warm, if you can

The note matters most

A handwritten note that says "Thinking of you all, no need to reply" takes the pressure off. People in hospital rooms get too many messages to answer. Let them off the hook.

What not to send

  • Flowers to ICUs and many wards; check, because many hospitals no longer allow them
  • Large quantities that will go to waste
  • Anything with alcohol
  • Food that needs a fridge, unless you know there is one

FAQs

Questions people ask

What food can I take to someone in hospital?

First check with the family what the patient is allowed to eat. If they can eat normally, take small amounts of gentle food like soft bread, plain cake, idiyappam, rusks or easy-to-eat fruit.

What is a good get-well gift for someone recovering at home?

Easy, gentle food in small amounts, such as soft breads, mild cakes, idiyappam or idli, along with fruit. A basket for the family helps too, as they often have no time to cook.

Can I send cake to a hospital patient?

A small, plain cake such as banana bread or tea cake can be fine if the patient's diet allows it. Avoid cream cakes, which need refrigeration and are often too rich.
