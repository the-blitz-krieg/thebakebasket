Is It Safe to Order From a Home Baker? How to Check FSSAI and Hygiene

PBy Priya ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Yes, a registered home baker can be every bit as safe as a shop, and often fresher. Check three things: that the baker has an FSSAI registration (a 14-digit number you can verify on the FSSAI FoSCoS portal), that they can tell you plainly how they handle hygiene and storage, and that they're open about ingredients and allergens. If a baker won't answer those questions, order elsewhere.

I get asked this, gently, by new customers. I'm always glad. It means they care about what they're feeding their family, which is exactly how I feel about it. Our FAQ has our own answers.

Why home bakers need FSSAI registration

In India, anyone running a food business, including from home, needs to be registered with the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI). Small home bakers typically hold a Basic Registration; larger operations need a State or Central licence. In Tamil Nadu, the state's Food Safety Department, under the Commissioner of Food Safety, administers registration and enforcement. Delivery apps also require an FSSAI number before a baker can list.

The thresholds and rules have been updated over the years, so check the current requirements on the FSSAI website rather than relying on older articles.

How to check a baker's FSSAI number

  1. Ask the baker for their 14-digit FSSAI registration or licence number. It should be on their website, social profile or packaging.
  2. Go to the FSSAI FoSCoS portal (foscos.fssai.gov.in).
  3. Use the "FBO search" option and enter the number.
  4. Check that the name and address match the baker, and that the registration is active.

That takes two minutes and tells you the business is on the record.

What registration does and doesn't tell you

Registration means the baker has declared their business and agreed to follow food safety rules. It doesn't mean someone inspects every kitchen every week. So it's the starting point, not the whole answer.

Questions worth asking

Any good home baker will be happy to answer these:

  • How do you store ingredients? Look for sealed containers, a dedicated fridge or shelf, attention to expiry dates.
  • How do you keep the baking area clean? Clean, dedicated surfaces; hair covered; hands washed; pets kept out of the kitchen.
  • Do you bake to order, or ahead? Baking to order means fresher food.
  • How do you handle eggless and allergies? Separate utensils, honest answers about cross-contact.
  • How do you pack and transport? Food-grade boxes, cold handling for cream cakes.

Red flags

  • No FSSAI number, or reluctance to share it
  • Photos that are clearly someone else's (reverse-image search if unsure)
  • Claims that sound too good: "100% organic", "zero sugar", "chemical-free", with no detail
  • Prices far below everyone else's for the same thing
  • No clear answer about ingredients or allergens
  • Asking you to pay a different number or account from the one you've been ordering on (a common scam)

Why home bakers often win on freshness

A shop counter has to bake ahead. A home baker who bakes to order doesn't. Your cake might have been made this morning rather than two days ago. That's one of the main reasons families in Chennai have moved towards home bakers, along with the personal touch. More on that in why fresh-to-order matters.
