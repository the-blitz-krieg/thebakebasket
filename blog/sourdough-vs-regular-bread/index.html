Sourdough vs Regular Bread: Is Sourdough Really Better for You?

PBy Priya ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Real sourdough is leavened with a wild starter of flour and water instead of packaged yeast, and fermented slowly, often overnight. That long fermentation gives it a crisp crust, a chewy, open crumb and a gentle tang. Research suggests the fermentation can make sourdough easier for some people to digest and may give it a gentler effect on blood sugar than many white breads. It's a better everyday bread. It is not a health food, and it is not gluten-free.

I have a jar on my counter that I feed every day, like a slightly needy pet. That's my starter, and it's the whole difference between sourdough and the soft loaf in a plastic bag. You can see what we bake on our breads page.

What sourdough actually is

Most bread is made with commercial yeast, a single, fast-working strain that makes dough rise in an hour or two. Sourdough uses a "starter" instead: flour and water left to ferment until wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria settle in and multiply. Feed it regularly and it becomes a living culture.

When it's mixed into dough, the yeasts produce gas, which makes the bread rise, slowly. The bacteria produce lactic and acetic acids, which give the tang. A sourdough loaf might take 12 to 36 hours from mixing to baking.

What that slow fermentation changes

Flavour. Long fermentation develops complex, slightly sour, almost nutty flavours that fast bread can't have.

Texture. A thick, crackling crust and a chewy, glossy, open crumb with irregular holes.

Keeping. The acidity helps sourdough stay fresh longer than yeasted bread without preservatives. Mine is still good on day three.

Digestion, possibly. During long fermentation, some of the carbohydrates known as FODMAPs break down, and the bacteria partly break down some of the gluten proteins. Some people who find regular bread uncomfortable find sourdough easier. That's not the same as being safe for coeliac disease; it isn't.

Blood sugar, possibly. Several studies have found that sourdough bread produces a lower blood sugar response than comparable yeasted white bread, likely because of the acids. The effect varies with the flour, and a whole-grain loaf of any kind generally does better than white.

What it doesn't change

Sourdough made from white flour is still mostly refined carbohydrate. It has similar calories to regular bread. And it still contains gluten, even if some is broken down.

So: a better bread, not a magic one.

How to tell real sourdough from imitation

A lot of bread sold as sourdough in India is ordinary yeasted bread with a sour flavouring or a little starter added for show. Here's how to tell:

  • Ingredients. Real sourdough needs only flour, water and salt. If the label lists yeast, or vinegar, it's a shortcut.
  • Price and availability. A real sourdough takes a day or more and some skill. Very cheap sourdough available everywhere at any time is suspicious.
  • Crumb. Real sourdough has an irregular, open crumb and a shine on the inside. A tight, even, fluffy crumb suggests a fast yeasted dough.
  • Crust. Thick, dark, crackling, often with an "ear" where it was scored.
  • Taste. A gentle tang that grows as you chew. Not a sharp vinegar note.
  • Ask. "How old is your starter, and how long is the fermentation?" A real sourdough baker will happily tell you about both.

How to eat it

Thick slices, toasted, with butter. With a good curry, like a Kerala stew. As a sandwich bread for something substantial. Torn into a salad. Don't keep it in the fridge; it goes stale fast there. Wrap it in a cloth or bag at room temperature, and freeze slices you won't eat in two or three days.

FAQs

Questions people ask

Is sourdough bread healthier than regular bread?

Sourdough's long fermentation may make it easier to digest for some people and can give it a gentler blood sugar response than regular white bread. It still contains gluten and similar calories, so it's a better choice rather than a health food.

Is sourdough gluten-free?

No. Sourdough made from wheat, rye or barley contains gluten. Fermentation breaks down some gluten, but sourdough is not safe for people with coeliac disease.

How can I tell if sourdough is real?

Real sourdough contains only flour, water and salt, with no added yeast. It has a thick crust, an open, irregular crumb and a mild tang.
