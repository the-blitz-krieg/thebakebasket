Fudgy or Cakey? What Makes a Brownie a Brownie

PBy Priya ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Fudgy brownies have more fat and chocolate relative to flour, little or no raising agent, and are baked just until set. Cakey brownies have more flour, often baking powder, more beaten-in air, and a longer bake. The shiny, crackly top comes from dissolving sugar well into warm butter and eggs and beating it briefly before the flour goes in.

I have strong views on this. I make fudgy brownies, the kind that stick a little to your fingers and taste even better on day two. You can find them on our brownies and cookies page. But both types are real brownies, and a lot of people love the cakey kind. Here's what decides which one you get.

The ratio

Everything starts with the balance of fat (butter and chocolate) to flour.

Fudgy: lots of butter and chocolate, very little flour. The flour is there to hold it together, barely.

Cakey: more flour, less fat. The texture moves towards a chocolate cake.

Chewy: somewhere in between, often with more egg, brown sugar or a little oil.

Chocolate or cocoa

Melted chocolate adds fat and cocoa solids, which make a denser, richer, fudgier brownie. Cocoa powder adds intense chocolate flavour without the fat, so cocoa-only brownies tend to be cakier unless you add more butter. I use both: dark chocolate for texture, a spoonful of cocoa for depth.

Raising agents

Fudgy brownies usually have none. Cakey brownies often have baking powder, which lifts them into a lighter, airier crumb.

Eggs and beating

Eggs bind and add structure. Beating eggs with sugar for a long time adds air, which makes brownies cakier. For fudgy brownies, you beat just enough to dissolve the sugar and create the top, then fold the flour in gently.

That crackly top

This is the part people ask about most. The thin, papery, shiny crust that shatters when you cut it.

It comes from sugar dissolving well into the warm wet ingredients, then rising to the surface as the brownie bakes and setting into a thin meringue-like layer. The tricks:

  • Use warm melted butter and chocolate, so the sugar dissolves.
  • Beat the eggs and sugar for a minute or two until the mixture turns slightly glossy and pale.
  • Don't skimp on sugar; it's what forms the crust.

Baking time

The biggest mistake is overbaking. Brownies keep cooking from their own heat after they leave the oven. Take fudgy brownies out when the edges are set and the middle still looks slightly soft, and a skewer comes out with a few moist crumbs. Then let them cool fully before cutting. Cutting warm brownies tastes wonderful and looks like a landslide.

Day two is better

Fudgy brownies firm up and the flavours deepen overnight. If you can wait, wait. If you can't, nobody blames you.

Eggless and ragi brownies

Eggless brownies can be just as fudgy; curd or condensed milk takes the place of egg, and the high chocolate content does most of the work. Ragi brownies are naturally dense and earthy, which suits fudgy brownies perfectly. More on that in is ragi cake gluten-free.

FAQs

Questions people ask

What makes brownies fudgy instead of cakey?

Fudgy brownies have a higher ratio of butter and chocolate to flour, little or no baking powder, and a shorter baking time. Cakey brownies have more flour, often baking powder, and more air beaten in.

How do you get a crackly top on brownies?

Dissolve the sugar into warm melted butter and chocolate, beat with the eggs briefly until glossy, and use enough sugar. The sugar rises and forms a thin, shiny crust as it bakes.

Should brownies be gooey in the middle?

Fudgy brownies should be moist and dense in the middle but set, not raw. Take them out when a skewer comes out with a few moist crumbs, and let them cool before cutting.
