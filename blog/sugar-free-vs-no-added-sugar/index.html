Sugar-Free, No Added Sugar, Reduced Sugar: What the Labels Mean

BBy Beth MathewPublished 26 November 20263 min read

"Sugar-free" should mean the product has almost no sugar at all, usually because a substitute is used. "No added sugar" means no sugar was added beyond what's naturally in the ingredients, such as fruit or milk. "No refined sugar" means white sugar wasn't used, though jaggery, honey or dates might have been. "Reduced sugar" means less than the usual recipe. Four labels, four different things.

On packaged foods in India, terms like "sugar-free" and "low sugar" are defined in FSSAI's labelling and claims regulations, with specific limits. Home bakers selling unpackaged cakes don't always follow that framework strictly, and the words get used loosely. So it's worth knowing what to ask. Our healthy bakes are labelled with the plainest words we could find.

Sugar-free

On a packaged product, "sugar-free" has a strict regulatory meaning: very little sugar per serving. In a bakery, it usually means sugar has been replaced with a substitute: stevia, sucralose, erythritol, maltitol or a blend.

What to know: - The cake still contains flour, which your body turns into glucose. - Some substitutes, like maltitol, still raise blood sugar somewhat and can upset the stomach in larger amounts. - Others, like stevia and erythritol, have little effect on blood sugar but can taste different, sometimes with a slight cooling or bitter note.

Ask: "Which sweetener?"

No added sugar

Nothing sweet was added, but the ingredients may be naturally sweet. A banana cake with no added sugar gets its sweetness from very ripe bananas. A date cake gets it from dates.

What to know: fruit sugar is still sugar. A cake sweetened entirely with dates can be just as sweet, and contain nearly as much sugar, as one made with white sugar. The difference is that fruit brings fibre and other nutrients with it.

No refined sugar

This is the label The Bake Basket uses most for its wholesome bakes. It means no white sugar or refined sweeteners, but the cake is sweetened with jaggery, palm jaggery, coconut sugar, honey or dates.

What to know: these are all sugars. They're less processed and taste more interesting, but they affect your body in broadly similar ways. More on this in jaggery vs sugar in cakes.

Reduced sugar or less sugar

The recipe uses less sugar than the baker's standard version, often by a quarter to a third. It's a simple, honest option that many families like because the cake still tastes like the cake they know, just less sweet.

What to know: ask "reduced by how much?" A good baker will know.

A side-by-side

Label Sugar added? Natural sugars? Typical sweetener Good for
Sugar-free No (substitute used) Possibly, from flour and dairy Stevia, erythritol, sucralose, maltitol People strictly limiting sugar, with care
No added sugar No Yes Ripe fruit, dates Families who want natural sweetness
No refined sugar Yes, unrefined Yes Jaggery, honey, dates, coconut sugar Less processed treats with more flavour
Reduced sugar Yes, less Yes White sugar, in smaller amounts The familiar cake, gentler

Why bakers blur these

Mostly because "sugar-free" sells. It's an attractive phrase, and it's easy to stretch. But families who are managing a parent's diabetes, or cutting down for their own health, deserve to know exactly what they're eating. Misleading them, even with good intentions, can do harm.

So the rule in Priya's kitchen is simple. If it has jaggery, the label says jaggery. If it has less sugar, it says how much less. "Sugar-free" is never used unless it truly is.

FAQs

Questions people ask

What is the difference between sugar-free and no added sugar?

Sugar-free means the product contains almost no sugar, usually because a sugar substitute was used. No added sugar means no sugar was added, but natural sugars from fruit, milk or dates are still present.

Does no refined sugar mean healthy?

No refined sugar means white sugar wasn't used, but the cake is usually sweetened with jaggery, honey or dates, which are still sugars. It's less processed, not sugar-free.

What does reduced sugar mean in a cake?

Reduced sugar means the cake has less sugar than the baker's standard recipe, often a quarter to a third less. Ask the baker for the exact reduction.
