Jaggery vs Sugar in Cakes: Is a Jaggery Cake Healthier?

BBy Beth MathewPublished 26 November 20263 min read

A jaggery cake is a better-quality treat, not a low-sugar one. Jaggery is less refined than white sugar and keeps small amounts of minerals such as iron and potassium, and it gives cakes a lovely caramel depth. But it is still mostly sugar, with a similar calorie count, and it raises blood glucose in much the same way. Choose jaggery for flavour and for less processing. Don't choose it as diabetic food.

That isn't what everyone wants to hear, so let me explain properly, and then explain why Priya still loves baking with it. You'll find our jaggery bakes on the healthy bakes page.

What jaggery is

Jaggery (vellam in Tamil, sharkara in Malayalam) is made by boiling sugarcane juice, or the sap of palms, until it thickens and sets. Because it isn't refined the way white sugar is, some of the molasses and minerals stay in. That's what gives it its colour, its smell and its flavour.

Palm jaggery (karupatti) comes from palm sap and has a smokier, deeper taste. Nattu sakkarai, often sold as country sugar, is a granulated, less refined cane sugar that behaves a little more like brown sugar in baking.

What it adds

Flavour. This is the real reason to bake with it. Jaggery brings caramel, toffee and a faint smokiness. In a banana loaf, a date cake or a ragi brownie, it tastes like it belongs.

Small amounts of minerals. Jaggery contains some iron, potassium and magnesium that refined sugar loses. The amounts in a slice of cake are small, but it's something.

Less processing. For many families, choosing ingredients closer to how they come from the farm matters, and jaggery fits that.

What it doesn't change

Calories. Gram for gram, jaggery has roughly the same calories as white sugar.

Blood sugar. Jaggery is mainly sucrose, the same sugar as white sugar, with some glucose and fructose. Its effect on blood glucose is broadly similar. There's no strong evidence that it's safe for people with diabetes in amounts where white sugar wouldn't be.

The fact that it's a treat. A jaggery cake is still cake.

How it behaves in baking

Jaggery is a little trickier than sugar. It's moist and variable, so batches differ in sweetness and colour. It can make cakes denser and darker. It caramelises faster, so cakes brown sooner. And it pairs better with some flavours than others.

Brilliant with: banana, dates, ragi, wheat, coconut, cardamom, dry ginger, chocolate, coffee, walnuts. Less good with: delicate vanilla sponges, light fruit cakes, anything meant to be pale and airy.

Who should be careful

Anyone with diabetes, prediabetes or insulin resistance should treat a jaggery cake like any other cake: a small piece, ideally after a meal, and in line with their doctor's advice. If that's the situation in your family, read our post on choosing a cake when someone has diabetes.

And for babies under one, the general advice is to avoid added sugars of any kind, jaggery included.

So why bake with it at all?

Because it tastes like home. Because it makes a better cake in the right recipe. And because for a family trying to eat a little more simply, swapping refined sugar for jaggery in an occasional treat is a reasonable, honest step, as long as no one is told it's a health food.

That's how The Bake Basket labels its jaggery bakes: no refined sugar. Never sugar-free, because they aren't.

FAQs

Questions people ask

Is jaggery cake good for health?

Jaggery cake is less processed than a white-sugar cake and contains small amounts of minerals, but it has similar calories and still raises blood sugar. It's a better treat, not a health food.

Is jaggery better than sugar for diabetics?

No. Jaggery is mostly sucrose and affects blood glucose much like white sugar. People with diabetes should limit it in the same way and follow medical advice.

Does jaggery cake taste different?

Yes. It's darker and has a caramel, toffee-like flavour. It suits banana, date, ragi, coconut and chocolate cakes especially well.
