What Replaces Eggs in an Eggless Cake, and Why It's Still Soft

PBy Priya ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

In an eggless cake, curd (yogurt), milk, condensed milk, oil or butter, and a balanced mix of baking powder and baking soda take over the jobs eggs normally do: binding, rising, moisture and richness. Done carefully, an eggless sponge is just as soft as one with eggs. Done carelessly, it's dense and gummy, which is why eggless cakes got a bad name.

I bake a lot of eggless cakes. In Chennai, for many families, an eggless cake is simply what a cake is. So I've spent a long time learning how to make them stand on their own.

What eggs actually do

To replace something properly you need to know what it was doing. In a cake, eggs do four things.

They bind. Egg proteins set as they heat, holding the crumb together so the cake slices instead of falling apart.

They help it rise. Beaten eggs trap air, and that air expands in the oven. Eggs also turn to steam, which lifts the cake.

They add moisture and fat. The yolk especially brings richness and a tender texture.

They emulsify. Yolks help fat and water mix smoothly, giving a fine, even crumb.

An eggless cake has to cover all four.

What does the work instead

Curd (yogurt). My favourite. It adds moisture and a gentle tang, and its acidity reacts with baking soda to produce lift. Cakes made with curd have a soft, slightly springy crumb, almost velvety. It's also an ingredient every Chennai kitchen already has.

Milk and a little vinegar or lemon. The same idea as buttermilk. The acid helps the raising agents and keeps the cake tender.

Condensed milk. Adds richness, sweetness and binding. Wonderful in vanilla and butterscotch cakes, but it's sweet, so the sugar is reduced to balance it.

Oil or butter. Oil keeps an eggless cake moist for longer, which is why many eggless chocolate cakes use it. Butter gives better flavour. I often use both.

Baking powder and baking soda, measured carefully. Too little and the cake is heavy. Too much and it rises fast, collapses, and tastes faintly soapy. This is where most bad eggless cakes go wrong.

Mashed banana, applesauce, flaxseed. Useful in some bakes, especially wholesome ones like banana bread, but they bring their own flavour and texture, so I use them selectively.

Why some eggless cakes turn out dense

If you've had a heavy eggless cake, it was probably one of these:

  • Overmixing. Without eggs, gluten develops more easily. Mix too long and the cake turns chewy.
  • Too much raising agent, which makes it rise and sink.
  • The wrong flour balance. Eggless sponges are more delicate; they need a lighter hand.
  • Baking too hot, which sets the outside before the inside rises.
  • A recipe that just "took the eggs out" of an egg cake without rebalancing everything else.

Does it taste different?

Slightly, and often in a good way. Curd-based eggless sponges have a faint tang that's lovely with fruit and cream. Chocolate eggless cakes are very hard to tell apart from egg-based ones; the cocoa takes centre stage. Where you'll notice the difference most is in very light, airy sponges like a classic chiffon, which rely heavily on whipped egg. For those, I adjust the recipe rather than pretend.

Eggless doesn't mean vegan, or allergy-safe

Two things worth knowing.

Eggless bakes usually contain milk, butter and curd, so they aren't vegan. And most home kitchens, including mine, also bake with eggs. I use separate bowls and wash everything carefully between batches, but if someone has a severe egg allergy, please tell me before ordering so we can talk about it properly. FAQs

Questions people ask

What is used instead of eggs in eggless cakes?

Eggless cakes usually use curd (yogurt), milk with a little vinegar or lemon, condensed milk, oil or butter, and a careful balance of baking powder and baking soda to replace what eggs do.

Are eggless cakes as soft as normal cakes?

Yes, when made well. Curd and milk give a tender, moist crumb, and many people can't tell a good eggless cake from one made with eggs.

Is eggless cake healthier than egg cake?

Not necessarily. Eggless cakes contain similar amounts of sugar and fat. They're chosen for dietary, religious or allergy reasons rather than health.
