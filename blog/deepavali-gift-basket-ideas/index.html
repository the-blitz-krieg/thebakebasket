A Deepavali Basket That Isn't Just Sweets

PBy Prisha ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

The best Deepavali baskets balance sweet with savoury and traditional with something a little new: a tin of nankhatai or coconut cookies, a box of brownies, a small cake or loaf, something savoury like jeera cookies or cheese straws, and a handwritten note. It stands out because every house already gets six boxes of the same mithai. Order at least a week ahead, and three to four weeks for office quantities.

You can see what goes into ours on the gift hampers page.

The sweet-box problem

By Deepavali evening, most Chennai kitchens have a counter stacked with boxes: Mysore pak, kaju katli, laddoos, a bit of everything. They're lovely. They're also, by the fourth box, a little indistinguishable. Half end up re-gifted. Some end up in the fridge until January.

A basket that looks and tastes different gets noticed. It's opened first, not last.

What to put in

A good basket has a mix, roughly in these parts:

Something traditional. Nankhatai, coconut cookies, cashew cookies, or a few pieces of homemade mithai. It's Deepavali; tradition matters.

Something rich. A box of fudgy brownies or a small chocolate cake. The children of the house will find this first.

Something savoury. Jeera cookies, cheese straws, savoury biscuits. Essential for the uncle who "doesn't eat sweets" and for the evening after three days of sugar.

Something for the morning after. A loaf of brioche, banana bread or a fruit cake for breakfast.

Something lighter. A few millet or jaggery bakes for anyone watching sugar. More thoughtful than it sounds.

A personal note. Handwritten, with the family's name.

By recipient

Your parents or in-laws. Traditional cookies, a fruit cake, millet bakes if anyone watches sugar, and something nostalgic like coconut buns.

Neighbours. Smaller baskets with cookies and brownies. In apartment blocks, consistency matters more than size; everyone on the floor should get the same.

Your domestic help, security and drivers. A generous, practical basket: a cake, cookies and savoury snacks for their whole family. Often more appreciated than anything else you give.

Colleagues and your team. Individual boxes, or one large basket for the office pantry. Read corporate Deepavali gifting for more.

Clients. A premium basket with your card, delivered to their home rather than the office, because that's where Deepavali happens.

A few rules

  • Label everything, especially anything with nuts or egg.
  • Avoid cream-based items; baskets sit around for a day or two.
  • Choose bakes that keep for at least three or four days.
  • Don't overfill; a basket that looks abundant but tidy is better than one that's crammed.
  • Use a real basket if you can. People keep them, and reuse them, and remember who gave them.

FAQs

Questions people ask

What should I put in a Deepavali gift basket?

Mix traditional cookies or sweets, something rich like brownies, something savoury, a breakfast bake such as fruit cake or brioche, and a handwritten note. Add millet or jaggery bakes for anyone watching sugar.

When should I order Deepavali hampers in Chennai?

Order family hampers at least a week ahead, and corporate or bulk hampers three to four weeks before Deepavali.

How long do Deepavali hamper items last?

Cookies and brownies usually last four to seven days, fruit cakes about a week or more. Avoid cream-based items in gift baskets.
