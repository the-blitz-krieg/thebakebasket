How Much Cake Do You Need? A Serving Guide for Every Party Size

PBy Prisha ShellPublished 26 November 20264 min read

For most parties, plan on about 100 grams of cake per guest. That means a 1 kg cake for 10 to 12 people, 2 kg for 20 to 25, and 3 kg for around 30 to 35. The rest of this post explains when to round up, when you can safely round down, and why the answer for a children's party is not the same as the answer for a wedding reception.

If you already know your numbers and just want to talk flavours, our custom cakes page has everything else.

The quick table

Guests Cake weight Notes
2 to 4 Bento cake (300 to 400 g) Perfect for couples, small families, "just us" evenings
5 to 6 500 g A small family birthday
10 to 12 1 kg The most common size we bake
15 to 18 1.5 kg Medium gatherings
20 to 25 2 kg Usually still a single tier
30 to 35 3 kg Often better as two tiers
40 to 50 4 to 5 kg Tiered, or one cake plus cupcakes
75 to 100 7 to 10 kg Tiered display cake, sometimes with a kitchen cake for cutting

These are party slices, roughly 80 to 100 g each. Wedding-style "finger slices" are smaller, closer to 50 g, which is why a wedding cake can stretch further than a birthday cake of the same weight.

When to round up

Children's parties. You'd think little people eat little slices. They don't, partly because the slice is mostly frosting and they want another one, and partly because parents end up eating a slice "to help finish it". For a party of 15 children and 10 adults, I'd plan for 25 full servings and add half a kilo.

When cake is the main event. An afternoon birthday at home, with snacks and tea rather than a meal, means people come back for seconds. Add 20%.

Teenagers. Enough said.

If you want leftovers. Some families want a slice for the next morning and a box to send to the neighbour. Decide that up front, because it's cheaper to bake a bigger cake than a second one.

When you can round down

After a heavy dinner. If the cake comes out after biryani and two kinds of dessert, many guests will take a sliver or skip it. You can plan on 70% of your headcount.

Rich cakes. A dense chocolate truffle, a cheesecake or a rich plum cake is cut thinner than a light vanilla sponge. People simply don't want a big piece. A 1 kg cheesecake can serve 14 easily.

Mostly older guests. Many will want a small piece, especially if there are diabetic family members. This is where a smaller cake plus a tray of reduced-sugar or millet bakes works beautifully.

Cupcakes change the maths

Cupcakes are one serving each, which makes planning easy and portion control automatic. For school birthdays, cupcakes are often better than a big cake because the teacher doesn't have to cut and plate anything. Count one per child plus a few extra for staff.

A popular combination for larger parties: a smaller "cutting cake" for the photos and candles, then cupcakes for serving. A 1.5 kg cake plus 36 cupcakes looks generous for 50 guests, costs less than a 5 kg tiered cake, and nobody waits in a queue for a slice.

Office parties

At work, people take a slice, chat, and go back to their desks. Plan on 80 g per person and choose a flavour almost everyone likes. Chocolate truffle and butterscotch are the safest. Ask about eggless needs before you order; in most Chennai offices, the eggless question decides the order, so many teams simply go fully eggless. Our corporate orders page has more on this.

A note on tiers

People often assume a tiered cake means a bigger cake. Not always. Tiers are about height and drama as much as quantity. A 3 kg cake can be a wide single tier or a taller two-tier. If you're expecting 30 guests and want a cake that photographs well, two tiers will look grander. If the cake is mostly for eating, one wide tier is easier to cut and serve.

The real question

When I talk to people planning a party, the number of guests is usually the easy part. The trickier question is how much of a moment the cake should be. Is it something everyone gathers around while the lights go off and the song starts? Or is it dessert after dinner?

If it's the moment, go a little bigger and make it beautiful. If it's dessert, buy for appetite, not for show.

FAQs

Questions people ask

How many people does a 1 kg cake serve?

A 1 kg cake serves about 10 to 12 people with standard party slices, or up to 15 to 18 with thin wedding-style slices.

How much cake do I need for 50 guests?

For 50 guests, plan on 4 to 5 kg of cake, usually as a two- or three-tier cake. A smaller cutting cake plus cupcakes is a popular, cheaper alternative.

Should I order extra cake for a kids' birthday party?

Yes. Children often take second helpings and parents eat too, so count every child and adult as a full serving and add about half a kilo.
