What to Take When Visiting a New Baby

PBy Prisha ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Take food for the parents, not a toy for the baby. New parents in the first few weeks are tired, often unable to cook, and receiving a stream of visitors. A basket of easy, one-handed food (banana bread, savoury muffins, soft bread, cookies) helps more than another soft toy. Message before you go, keep the visit short, and don't expect to hold the baby.

Our new baby baskets are built around exactly this.

Why food beats toys

Babies get dozens of toys and outfits in the first month. Many go unused, and most are outgrown in weeks. Food, on the other hand, solves an immediate problem: the parents need to eat, and they have neither the time nor the energy.

A new mother breastfeeding at 3 am will be grateful for a muffin she can eat with one hand. A father who has been in and out of the pharmacy all day will be grateful for a loaf of bread and something to put on it.

What to put in

One-handed snacks. Muffins (sweet and savoury), banana bread, cookies, date and nut bars. Things you can eat while holding a baby.

Breakfast. Soft bread, a whole-wheat loaf, brioche. Breakfast is often the meal that disappears entirely in the newborn weeks.

Something for visitors. A cake or cookie tin to offer the endless stream of relatives, so the parents don't have to go out and buy something.

Something nourishing. Many Indian families have traditional postpartum foods: ragi, sesame, ghee, dry ginger, garlic. If you know the family's traditions, ask before bringing these, as the grandmothers usually have strong views.

Ask about diet

New mothers in many Indian families follow a postpartum diet for several weeks, avoiding certain foods. Some avoid cold food, some avoid certain vegetables, many eat specific confinement foods. Ask a family member what's welcome before you send something.

Other useful gifts

  • Ready meals or a food delivery voucher
  • An offer to run an errand
  • A pack of the nappies they use (ask the size)
  • Something for older siblings, who often feel left out
  • A small gift for the mother herself: a book, a nice shawl, a skincare treat

Visiting etiquette

  • Message first. Always. And accept "not this week" gracefully.
  • Keep it short. Twenty to thirty minutes is plenty.
  • Wash your hands as soon as you arrive.
  • Don't visit if you're unwell, even a mild cold.
  • Don't expect to hold the baby. If you're offered, lovely.
  • Offer to help. Wash a few dishes, fold some laundry, make tea.
  • Don't give unsolicited advice. They're getting plenty.

If you can't visit

Send the basket anyway. A delivery with a handwritten note that says "Congratulations, no need to reply" is one of the kindest things you can do.

FAQs

Questions people ask

What should I bring when visiting a newborn?

Bring food for the parents, such as banana bread, savoury muffins, soft bread or cookies, which can be eaten easily with one hand. Check with the family before bringing anything, and keep the visit short.

What is a good gift for new parents in India?

Easy food baskets, ready meals, practical items like nappies, and a small gift for the mother are all appreciated. Ask about postpartum diet traditions before sending food.

Is it okay to visit a newborn in the first week?

Only if the parents invite you. Many families prefer fewer visitors in the first weeks. Always message first and never visit if you're unwell.
