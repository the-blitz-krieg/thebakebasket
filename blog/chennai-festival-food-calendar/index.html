Chennai's Festival Food Calendar, From Pongal to Christmas

PBy Prisha ShellPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Chennai's year runs on festival food. January brings sweet pongal; spring brings Ramzan's sharing plates, Tamil New Year and Vishu; August and September bring kozhukattai for Vinayagar Chathurthi and the Onam sadhya; Navaratri brings sundal; Deepavali brings sweets and gift baskets; December brings plum cake and vattayappam. Here's the year, month by month, with what to eat and when to order.

Festival dates move with the lunar and solar calendars, so always check the year's exact date. Our traditional steamed foods and gift hampers follow this calendar.

January: Pongal

Pongal, the Tamil harvest festival, runs over four days in mid-January: Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Mattu Pongal and Kaanum Pongal. The food is the festival's name: pongal, rice cooked in a new pot until it boils over, a sign of abundance. Sakkarai pongal (sweet, with jaggery, ghee, cashews and cardamom) and ven pongal (savoury, with pepper and cumin) both appear, along with sugarcane, turmeric plants and a great deal of goodwill.

Order ahead: baskets for family and colleagues a week before.

February: Valentine's Day

Not a traditional festival, but a busy date for small cakes, bento cakes and cupcake boxes.

Order ahead: two or three days; earlier if it falls on a weekend.

Ramzan and Eid (dates shift each year)

Through Ramzan, evenings in Chennai's Muslim neighbourhoods smell of nombu kanji, the rice porridge distributed at mosques for iftar. Samosas, dates, fruit and sharing plates go to neighbours. Eid ul-Fitr brings biryani, sheer khurma and plates of sweets sent across the street, often to Hindu and Christian neighbours, who send plates back at their own festivals.

Order ahead: sharing boxes and Eid baskets a week ahead.

March or April: Easter

In Kerala and Tamil Christian homes, Easter ends the Lenten fast. Appam and stew return to breakfast, along with vattayappam, and lamb or chicken for lunch.

Order ahead: vattayappam two or three days before.

April: Tamil New Year and Vishu

Tamil New Year (Puthandu), usually on 14 April, is marked with a meal that includes mango pachadi, a dish that's sweet, sour, bitter and spicy at once, a reminder that a year holds all of them. Kerala's Vishu falls around the same day, with the Vishukkani arrangement at dawn and a festive sadhya.

May: Mother's Day

A gentle, busy day for cakes, cupcake bouquets and baskets for mothers and grandmothers.

June: back to school

Lunchbox bakes and class birthday cupcakes. See lunchbox bakes kids will eat.

August or September: Vinayagar Chathurthi

Clay Vinayagar idols arrive home, and kitchens fill with steam. Sweet and savoury kozhukattai, modak, sundal and fruit are offered before anyone eats. See kozhukattai for Vinayagar Chathurthi.

Order ahead: at least a week before.

August or September: Onam

The Malayali harvest festival, ten days long, ending in Thiruvonam. The sadhya, two dozen or more dishes on a banana leaf, is the centre, with payasams to finish. Ela ada and banana chips are close to hand. See ela ada.

Order ahead: sadhyas from caterers two weeks before; snacks and ada a few days before.

September or October: Navaratri

Nine nights of golu, the stepped display of dolls, and visiting. Every evening a different sundal: chickpea, green gram, peanut, black-eyed bean. Small sweets and return gifts (thamboolam) for women and girls who visit.

Order ahead: thamboolam packs and small sweet boxes a week before the first evening.

October or November: Deepavali

The biggest gifting week of the year. Sweets, savouries, new clothes, early-morning oil baths, and baskets carried to family, neighbours and colleagues. See a Deepavali basket that isn't just sweets.

Order ahead: family baskets a week before; corporate orders three to four weeks before.

December: Christmas and New Year

Plum cake, vattayappam, rose cookies, kalkal, appam and stew, biryani for lunch, and stars on balconies across the city. New Year's Eve brings parties and late cake orders. See Christmas food in Chennai homes.

Order ahead: plum cake by early to mid November.

FAQs

Questions people ask

What are the main festival foods in Chennai?

Key festival foods include sweet and savoury pongal for Pongal, kozhukattai for Vinayagar Chathurthi, sundal for Navaratri, sweets and savouries for Deepavali, sheer khurma and biryani for Eid, ela ada and payasam for Onam, and plum cake and vattayappam for Christmas.

When should I order festival sweets in Chennai?

For most festivals, order at least a week ahead. For Deepavali corporate gifting and Christmas plum cakes, book three to six weeks ahead.

What is eaten on Tamil New Year?

Tamil New Year meals often include mango pachadi, which combines sweet, sour, bitter and spicy tastes, along with vadai, payasam and a festive vegetarian meal.
