Planning a Kids' Theme Cake That Tastes as Good as It Looks

PBy Priya ShellPublished 26 November 20264 min read

Start with the child, not the picture. Ask what they love this year, choose two or three colours and one hero element, and let the baker build the rest around a flavour your guests will actually eat. Most disappointing theme cakes go wrong because they try to fit a whole film onto the top of a 1 kg cake.

Dinosaurs, rockets, unicorns, cricket pitches, mermaids, construction sites: every child has a world they want on their cake. Here's how I plan them so the six-year-old gasps and the adults ask for a second slice. If you'd like to see options first, our custom cakes page has more.

Start with one hero

Every good theme cake has one thing your eye goes to first. A dinosaur. A rocket. A crown. A cricket bat crossed with a stump.

Everything else supports it: the colours, a few small details, the child's name and age. When there's no hero, the cake turns into a crowded collage. When there are three heroes, they fight for space and none of them look right.

So if your daughter loves unicorns, rainbows, mermaids and kittens, ask her which one is the birthday. The others can go on the plates.

Pick the colours early

Two or three colours, maybe four. That's it. Give them as real examples: "the blue of her school uniform", "the green in the party banner". Bright colours like deep red, navy and black need a lot of colouring, which can affect taste, so I tend to use them only for accents. Pastels and soft brights photograph beautifully and taste the same as a plain cake.

Let the finish follow the design

This is where theme cakes often go wrong. Parents ask for a fully fondant-covered cake because that's what the pictures online show, and then half the children peel off the fondant and the cake looks like a crime scene by the second slice.

My rule of thumb:

  • Buttercream or cream finish for the cake itself. It tastes good and children eat it.
  • Fondant only for figures and toppers: the dinosaur, the crown, the number. These can be made ahead and kept as a souvenir.
  • Printed edible images only where a flat picture is the point. They're quick but look flat next to piped work.

There's a longer explanation in fondant vs buttercream vs fresh cream.

About characters

Many children ask for a favourite cartoon or film character. I'm happy to make a cake inspired by the theme, using the colours, the setting, the props and the feeling, without copying a trademarked character exactly. A "space adventure" cake with a rocket, planets and your child's name is still their dream cake, and often looks more special than a copy of a toy box.

Choose a flavour for the guests

The child will eat a few bites of frosting and wander off to play. The adults and older children will eat the cake. So pick the flavour for them.

Chocolate truffle, butterscotch and vanilla with fresh fruit are the safest choices for a mixed crowd. If several guests are eggless, consider making the whole cake eggless so nobody gets a different slice.

Size: count everybody

Count every child and every adult. Children's parties eat more cake than you expect. For 20 children and 15 adults, I'd plan 3 to 3.5 kg, or a 2 kg theme cake plus cupcakes in matching colours. See how much cake do you need.

What to send your baker

Make it easy and you'll get a better cake:

  1. Child's name, age and the date
  2. The theme, in one sentence
  3. The hero element
  4. Colours
  5. Two or three reference pictures, plus a photo of the invitation or decorations
  6. Guest count
  7. Flavour, eggless or not, any allergies
  8. Where the cake will be and when it'll be cut

On the day

Keep the cake in the fridge until about 20 minutes before cutting, or in an air-conditioned room if it has a lot of fondant. Place it away from windows and the hall's spotlights. Take the photos first, then cut. And save the fondant figure. A surprising number of them end up on a shelf for years.

FAQs

Questions people ask

How far in advance should I order a kids' theme cake?

At The Bake Basket, order at least 2 days ahead for a buttercream theme cake, and about a week ahead if the design has fondant figures that need time to set.

Can you make a cake with a cartoon character?

We make cakes inspired by your child's favourite theme, with matching colours, settings and props. We don't copy trademarked characters exactly, but the cake will still feel like their favourite story.

What's the best cake for a kids' party?

Chocolate truffle, vanilla with fresh fruit and butterscotch are the most popular. Choose a buttercream or cream finish so the children actually eat the cake, and use fondant only for toppers.
