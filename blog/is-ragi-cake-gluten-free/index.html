Is Ragi Cake Gluten-Free? What Millet Bakes Really Are

BBy Beth MathewPublished 26 November 20263 min read

Ragi (finger millet) is naturally gluten-free. Most ragi cakes are not. To get a soft, cake-like texture, the majority of ragi recipes mix ragi flour with wheat flour or maida, sometimes as much as half and half. A ragi cake is gluten-free only if it's made with no wheat at all and in conditions that avoid cross-contact, so if gluten matters to you, ask.

That's the headline. The rest of this post is about why ragi is still worth eating in a cake, and how to choose millet bakes with your eyes open. If you're looking to order, our healthy bakes page lists what we make.

What ragi is

Ragi, kezhvaragu in Tamil, finger millet in English, is one of the oldest grains in South Indian kitchens. Ragi koozh in summer, ragi kali in villages, ragi dosai, ragi puttu. Many of our grandparents ate it daily.

Nutritionally, it's notable for its calcium content, which is high for a grain, and it's a good source of dietary fibre. It has a deep, earthy, slightly nutty taste and a reddish-brown colour that looks almost like cocoa.

Why bakers mix it with wheat

Gluten is the protein network in wheat that gives bread its chew and cake its structure. Ragi has none. On its own, ragi flour makes a cake that's tender, crumbly and dense, a bit like a very soft fudge. That's wonderful in a brownie, which is supposed to be dense. It's harder in a light birthday sponge.

So most recipes add some wheat flour, whole-wheat atta or maida to help it hold together and rise. The result tastes of ragi, has more fibre than a plain cake, and is no longer gluten-free.

Where ragi shines

Brownies. This is where ragi is at its best. Its natural density and earthy flavour sit beautifully with dark chocolate, and children usually can't tell. A ragi brownie can be made with no wheat at all.

Cookies. Ragi cookies with jaggery and a little ghee are crisp, nutty and very moreish.

Banana bread and loaf cakes. Ragi with banana and whole wheat makes a moist, filling tea-time cake.

Puttu and steamed foods. Ragi puttu needs no baking at all, and is naturally gluten-free.

What "ragi cake" can mean on a menu

Label What it might contain
Ragi cake Usually ragi plus wheat or maida
Ragi and whole-wheat cake Ragi plus atta, no maida
100% ragi cake Ragi as the only flour; check binders and cross-contact
Gluten-free ragi cake Should contain no wheat, barley or rye; ask about the kitchen
Ragi brownie Often 100% ragi, sometimes mixed

None of these are wrong. They're just different. What matters is that the label tells you which one you're getting.

Is a ragi cake healthier?

Healthier than a plain maida cake, in some ways. More fibre, more minerals, often more filling. But it's still cake. It still has sugar (or jaggery, which is also sugar; more on that in jaggery vs sugar), still has fat, and still deserves to be a treat rather than breakfast.

Where millet bakes really help is at a family table where different people have different needs. Appa watching his sugar, a teenager avoiding maida, a child who just likes chocolate. A ragi brownie can make all three happy.

If someone has coeliac disease

Coeliac disease is a medical condition where even small amounts of gluten cause harm. If someone in your family has it, ask your baker very specific questions: is the bake made with no wheat, barley or rye at all? Are oats used, and are they certified gluten-free? Are separate utensils and surfaces used? Is the kitchen also baking with wheat that day?

FAQs

Questions people ask

Is ragi gluten-free?

Yes, ragi (finger millet) is naturally gluten-free. But most ragi cakes are mixed with wheat flour for texture, so they are not gluten-free unless made specifically without wheat.

Is ragi cake good for health?

Ragi cake usually has more fibre and minerals than a plain maida cake and can be more filling, but it still contains sugar and fat, so it's best enjoyed as a treat.

Can people with gluten intolerance eat ragi brownies?

Only if the brownies are made with no wheat flour and the baker takes care to avoid cross-contact. Ask the baker exactly how they're made.
